## What surgeons look for

Laser vision correction reshapes the cornea, the clear front window of the eye, so it focuses light properly. To do that safely you need a stable prescription, a cornea thick and regular enough to reshape, a healthy tear film and no conditions that slow healing. A consultation measures all of this with corneal mapping (topography) and thickness scans.

## The main procedures

- **LASIK:** a thin flap is made, the laser reshapes the cornea beneath, and the flap is laid back. Fast recovery.
- **SMILE:** a small piece of tissue is removed through a tiny incision, with no flap.
- **PRK:** the surface layer is removed and grows back. Longer recovery, but no flap, so it suits thin corneas and contact sports.
- **ICL:** a lens is placed inside the eye in front of your natural lens. Suits very high prescriptions and thin corneas.
- **Refractive lens exchange:** the natural lens is replaced, usually for people over about 50.
