faq:
  - q: What do OD and OS mean?
    a: They come from Latin. OD (oculus dexter) is the right eye and OS (oculus sinister) is the left. OU means both eyes.
  - q: Is a higher number worse?
    a: Larger numbers mean a stronger correction, not unhealthy eyes. A minus SPH corrects nearsightedness and a plus SPH corrects farsightedness.
  - q: Where do I find my PD?
    a: It may be written on your prescription. If not, an optician can measure it, or some online stores measure it with your phone camera.

## The parts of a glasses prescription

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| SPH (sphere) | Main lens power in diopters. Minus for nearsightedness, plus for farsightedness |
| CYL (cylinder) | Extra power for astigmatism. Blank means none |
| AXIS | Direction of the astigmatism correction, 1 to 180 degrees |
| ADD | Extra magnifying power for reading, for bifocals or progressives |
| PRISM | Corrects eye alignment problems; less common |
| PD | Distance between your pupils in millimetres |

## Why two prescriptions can look different but be the same

Astigmatism can be written with a minus or a plus cylinder. The decoder shows your prescription in both forms, so you can compare a prescription from an optometrist with one from an ophthalmologist.

## Choosing lenses

Stronger prescriptions make thicker lenses, especially at the edges for minus lenses. High-index materials (1.60, 1.67, 1.74) keep lenses thinner and lighter. Polycarbonate or Trivex is safer for children and sports. An anti-reflective coating helps at night and on screens.

Glasses prescriptions usually expire after one or two years. Buying new glasses is a good moment to check that your eyes have not changed.
