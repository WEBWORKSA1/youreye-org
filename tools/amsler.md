## What the Amsler grid shows

The Amsler grid is a square of evenly spaced lines with a dot in the middle. While you look at the dot, the grid falls on the **macula**, the small central part of the retina that gives you sharp detail vision for reading and recognizing faces.

When the macula is damaged, straight lines can look bent or wavy (*metamorphopsia*), and patches can look blurred, grey or missing. The brain fills in gaps, so people often don't notice these changes in everyday life. Testing one eye at a time stops the stronger eye from hiding a problem in the weaker one.

## Who should use it

- Anyone over 50, especially with a family history of age-related macular degeneration (AMD)
- People diagnosed with dry or wet AMD
- People with diabetes or high nearsightedness, who are more likely to have retinal problems

## What to do with your result

If the grid looks normal, keep checking regularly. If you notice distortion for the first time, or a change from last time, contact an eye doctor promptly. **Sudden distortion or a dark spot is an urgent symptom** because wet AMD can be treated, and treatment works best when it starts early.
