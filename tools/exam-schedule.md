## Why regular exams matter even with good vision

Several of the leading causes of vision loss, including glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy and early macular degeneration, cause no symptoms at first. A comprehensive exam, often with dilating drops, lets the doctor see the retina and optic nerve and catch problems while treatment works best.

## General schedule

| Age | How often |
| --- | --- |
| 6 to 12 months | First comprehensive exam |
| 3 to 5 years | At least once |
| 6 to 17 years | Before first grade, then yearly |
| 18 to 64 years | At least every 2 years; yearly if at risk |
| 65 and older | Yearly |
| Diabetes, any age | Dilated exam at least yearly |

## Who counts as at risk

People with diabetes, high blood pressure, a family history of glaucoma or macular degeneration, high nearsightedness, contact lens wear, past eye surgery or injury, or long-term steroid use usually need more frequent exams. Your eye doctor will set the right interval for you.
