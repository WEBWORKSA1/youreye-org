## How the screener works

Each plate is a circle of colored dots. A number is hidden in dots of one color family, surrounded by dots of another family with similar brightness. People with typical color vision see the number clearly; people with certain color vision deficiencies see a jumble of dots or a different number.

The first plate uses light and dark grey, which nearly everyone can read. If you miss it, the problem is likely the screen or settings rather than your color vision.

## Types of color vision deficiency

- **Red-green (protan and deutan):** by far the most common, usually inherited on the X chromosome, which is why it affects many more men.
- **Blue-yellow (tritan):** rare, and more often caused by eye or nerve conditions or some medicines.
- **Complete color blindness (achromatopsia):** very rare, usually with poor vision and light sensitivity.

## Getting a reliable result

Turn off night mode, blue-light filters, color filters and true-tone settings. Use full brightness and look straight at the screen. Phone screens and older monitors can shift colors, so repeat on another device if your result surprises you.

## Why it matters

Color vision affects some careers and licences, including aviation, electrical work and some military and transport roles. Children with undiagnosed color deficiency can struggle with color-coded schoolwork, so let teachers know if your child is affected.
