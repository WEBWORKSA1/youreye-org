## Why contrast matters in real life

The world rarely looks like black letters on a white chart. Night driving, reading a faded receipt, seeing steps in dim light or recognizing a face in shadow all depend on contrast vision. People with reduced contrast sensitivity often say things look washed out or foggy even when their glasses are up to date.

## How this check works

One large letter appears at a time and gets fainter at each level, from full black down to under 1% contrast. Pick the letter you see from four choices. You need two correct answers to move down a level. Your score is shown as an approximate *log contrast sensitivity*, the scale clinics use with charts such as the Pelli-Robson chart.

## What lowers contrast vision

- Cataract, which scatters light inside the eye
- Glaucoma and other optic nerve conditions
- Macular degeneration and diabetic eye disease
- Dry eye and a poor tear film
- Dirty or scratched glasses, and glare

If faded vision or glare is affecting your driving at night, mention it at your eye exam even if you read the chart well.
