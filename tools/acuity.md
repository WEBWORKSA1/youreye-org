  - q: What does 20/20 vision mean?
    a: It means you can read at 20 feet what a person with typical vision can read at 20 feet. 20/40 means you need to be at 20 feet to read what a typical eye reads from 40 feet. In metric countries the same scale is written 6/6 and 6/12.
  - q: Is an online acuity check accurate?
    a: With screen calibration and the right distance it gives a useful estimate, but screen quality, lighting and guessing all add error. It cannot measure your prescription or check eye health.

## How this check works

Eye doctors measure sharpness of vision, or *visual acuity*, with letters that get smaller line by line. Each line is sized so that its letters fill a known angle at the eye. We use the **tumbling E**, the chart used for children and people who don't read the Latin alphabet: you only say which way the E points.

First you match a box on screen to a bank card, which tells us how many pixels make a millimetre on your display. Then we draw each E at the exact size it would be on a chart at your chosen distance. You need two correct answers out of three to pass a line.

## Getting a fair result

- Use a laptop or desktop screen at full brightness, in a well-lit room.
- Stand or sit at the distance you choose and keep it steady. Farther is more accurate.
- Wear your distance glasses or contacts, if you have them, to see how well corrected you are. Take them off to see your uncorrected vision.
- Cover one eye with your palm without pressing on it.

## Reading your score

| Score | What it usually means |
| --- | --- |
| 20/16 to 20/20 | Typical healthy distance vision |
| 20/25 to 20/32 | Slightly reduced; a small prescription change is common |
| 20/40 to 20/63 | Reduced; below many driving standards without correction |
| 20/80 or worse | Clearly reduced; book an exam soon |

A difference of two or more lines between your eyes, or any sudden drop, is worth checking even if one eye reads 20/20. A sharp-vision score also says nothing about glaucoma or other diseases that start without symptoms, which is why adults still need regular dilated exams.
