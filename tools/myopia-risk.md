## Why childhood myopia matters

Nearsightedness usually starts between ages 6 and 14 and tends to worsen until the late teens or early twenties. The earlier it starts, the stronger it often becomes. High myopia raises the lifetime risk of retinal detachment, glaucoma, cataract and myopic macular degeneration, so slowing it in childhood protects sight later.

## The risk factors in this check

- **Parents with myopia:** the risk rises with one parent and more with two
- **Little time outdoors:** daylight appears protective; about two hours a day is a common target
- **Lots of close work:** reading, tablets, phones and games at short distances
- **Early onset and fast progression:** glasses getting stronger each year

## Signs your child may not see well

Squinting, sitting close to the TV, holding devices very close, rubbing eyes, headaches, or trouble seeing the board at school. Many children do not complain because they think everyone sees the way they do.
