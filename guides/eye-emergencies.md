## If someone has sudden vision loss with stroke signs

Sudden vision loss together with facial drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech or confusion can be a stroke. **Call emergency services immediately.**

## First aid by situation

| Situation | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chemical splash | Flush the eye with clean lukewarm water for at least 15 minutes, holding the lids open, then get emergency care | Don't rub, and don't try to neutralize the chemical |
| Blow to the eye | Apply a cold compress gently; get checked if vision changes, the eye bleeds, or you see floaters | Don't press on the eye |
| Cut or puncture | Cover with a rigid shield and go to emergency | Don't remove a stuck object, rinse, or press |
| Dust or grit | Blink, lift the upper lid over the lower, rinse with water or saline | Don't rub or use tweezers |
| Painful red eye in a contact lens wearer | Remove the lens, see an eye doctor today | Don't put the lens back in |

## Symptoms that should never wait

Some eye problems can cause permanent vision loss within hours or days. A retinal detachment, acute angle-closure glaucoma, a serious corneal infection and a blocked blood vessel in the retina are all treatable, but only if seen quickly. If in doubt, get checked today.

## Prevention

- Wear safety glasses for DIY, yard work and chemical cleaners
- Use polycarbonate sports eyewear for racquet and ball sports
- Leave fireworks to professionals
- Keep laser pointers away from children
- Store household chemicals out of reach and point spray nozzles away from faces
