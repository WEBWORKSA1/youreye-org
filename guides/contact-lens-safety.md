## Why it matters

According to the CDC, about 45 million people in the US wear contact lenses. Most never have a problem, but lens wearers are at higher risk of **keratitis**, an infection of the cornea that can scar it and permanently reduce vision. Most serious infections are linked to habits that can be changed.

## The rules that prevent most infections

1. **Wash and dry your hands** before touching your lenses or eyes.
2. **Never sleep in lenses** unless your eye doctor prescribed lenses for overnight wear.
3. **Keep water away.** Do not rinse or store lenses in tap water, and do not swim or shower in lenses. Water can carry *Acanthamoeba*, which causes a severe infection.
4. **Use fresh solution every time.** Never top up old solution in the case.
5. **Rub and rinse** lenses if your solution instructions say to.
6. **Care for your case.** Rinse with solution, let it air-dry face down, and replace it at least every three months.
7. **Replace lenses on schedule.** Dailies are single-use. Two-weekly and monthly lenses should not be stretched.
8. **Keep a pair of glasses** so you can give your eyes a break.

## If your eye becomes red or painful

Take the lens out and do not put it back in. If pain, redness, blurred vision or light sensitivity do not improve quickly, see an eye doctor the same day and bring the lens and case. Do not use leftover antibiotic or steroid drops on your own.

## Regular check-ups

A yearly contact lens exam checks that lenses still fit and that the cornea is getting enough oxygen. Our [glasses-to-contacts converter](tools/contacts-converter.html) explains why contact prescriptions differ from glasses.
