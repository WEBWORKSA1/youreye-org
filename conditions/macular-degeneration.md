## What it is

The **macula** is a small area at the center of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed central vision. In age-related macular degeneration, the macula is damaged over time. The National Eye Institute describes AMD as the most common cause of vision loss for older adults.

There are two forms:

- **Dry AMD** is the most common. Yellow deposits called drusen build up and the macula thins slowly. Late dry AMD is called *geographic atrophy*.
- **Wet AMD** is less common but faster. Abnormal blood vessels grow under the retina and leak fluid or blood. It can cause sudden vision loss.

## Symptoms

- Blurry or fuzzy central vision
- Straight lines that look wavy
- A blank or dark spot in the center of vision
- Colors that look less bright
- Needing more light to read

Early AMD often has no symptoms.

## Who is at risk

Risk rises with age, especially after 55. Other risk factors include a family history of AMD, smoking, being white, high blood pressure and a diet low in leafy greens and fish.

## How it is diagnosed

A dilated eye exam, the Amsler grid, optical coherence tomography (OCT) and sometimes dye tests (angiography) show the macula in detail.

## Treatment

- **Intermediate dry AMD:** the AREDS2 vitamin and mineral formula can slow progression
- **Geographic atrophy:** newer injectable medicines can slow its growth for some people
- **Wet AMD:** anti-VEGF injections into the eye stop leaking vessels and often stabilize or improve vision. Starting early gives the best results
- **Low vision rehabilitation:** magnifiers, lighting, devices and training to make the most of remaining sight

## Prevention and monitoring

Do not smoke, eat leafy greens and fish, stay active and manage blood pressure. If you are at risk, check each eye regularly with our [Amsler grid](tools/amsler.html) and report changes promptly.
