## What it is

Pink eye, or conjunctivitis, is inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin clear layer covering the white of the eye and the inside of the eyelids. The blood vessels swell and the eye looks pink or red.

## Types and how to tell them apart

| Type | Typical signs | Contagious? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viral | Watery discharge, often starts in one eye and spreads, may come with a cold | Yes |
| Bacterial | Thick yellow or green discharge, crusted lids in the morning | Yes |
| Allergic | Very itchy, both eyes, watery, often with sneezing | No |
| Irritant | After smoke, chlorine or a chemical | No |

## Treatment

- **Viral:** usually clears on its own in one to two weeks. Cool compresses and artificial tears ease symptoms
- **Bacterial:** often mild and self-limiting; antibiotic drops can shorten it
- **Allergic:** antihistamine or mast-cell stabilizer drops and avoiding triggers
- **Chemical splash:** rinse with clean water for at least 15 minutes and get urgent care

Stop wearing contact lenses until the eye has fully cleared, and replace lenses and cases used during the infection.

## Prevention

Wash your hands often, avoid touching or rubbing your eyes, do not share towels, pillows, eye drops or makeup, and replace eye makeup after an infection.

## When it is not pink eye

Some serious conditions also cause a red eye, including corneal infections (especially in contact lens wearers), uveitis and acute glaucoma. Pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision are warning signs that need same-day care.
