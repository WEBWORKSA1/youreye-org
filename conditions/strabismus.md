## What it is

Strabismus is a misalignment of the eyes. While one eye looks straight ahead, the other turns:

- **Esotropia:** inward
- **Exotropia:** outward
- **Hypertropia and hypotropia:** up or down

The turn can be constant or come and go, for example when tired or daydreaming.

## Causes

In children, strabismus often runs in families and may relate to how the brain controls the eye muscles. Uncorrected farsightedness can cause *accommodative esotropia*, where the eyes cross from focusing hard. In adults, new strabismus can come from thyroid eye disease, diabetes, a stroke, nerve problems or an injury.

## Signs

- Eyes that do not appear to look in the same direction
- Squinting or shutting one eye in bright light
- Tilting or turning the head
- Double vision, mainly in adults

Some babies have wide nasal bridges that make the eyes look crossed when they are not (*pseudostrabismus*). An eye doctor can tell the difference.

## Why it matters

In children, the brain may ignore the turned eye, causing **amblyopia** and poor depth perception. In adults, strabismus causes double vision and can affect confidence and work.

## Treatment

- **Glasses**, which can fully correct some types
- **Patching or atropine drops** to treat amblyopia
- **Prism lenses** to reduce double vision
- **Eye muscle surgery** to realign the eyes
- **Botulinum toxin injections** in some cases

New double vision or a sudden eye turn in anyone can signal a nerve or brain problem and needs same-day medical care.
