## What it is

In an eye without astigmatism, the cornea is curved evenly in every direction, like a basketball. With astigmatism, it is curved more steeply in one direction, more like a rugby ball, or the lens inside the eye is shaped unevenly. Light focuses at two different points, so vision is blurred or stretched at every distance.

Astigmatism is very common. Most people have a small amount, and it often occurs together with nearsightedness or farsightedness.

## Symptoms

- Blurry or distorted vision, near and far
- Eye strain and headaches, especially after reading or screen work
- Squinting
- Trouble seeing at night, with streaks or halos around lights

## Who is at risk

Most astigmatism is present from birth and may run in families. It can also follow an eye injury, eye surgery or a corneal disease such as keratoconus.

## How it is diagnosed

An eye exam measures it with a refraction, a keratometer or a corneal map (topography). On a prescription it appears as the **CYL** (strength) and **AXIS** (direction) numbers. Our [prescription decoder](tools/prescription-decoder.html) explains them.

## Treatment

- **Glasses** with a cylindrical correction
- **Toric soft contact lenses**, which are weighted to stay in the right orientation
- **Rigid gas-permeable or scleral lenses**, which give the eye a smooth new front surface
- **Refractive surgery** such as LASIK, PRK or SMILE, or a toric implantable lens
- **Toric lens implants** during cataract surgery

Mild astigmatism with no symptoms may not need treatment at all.
