## What it is

High blood sugar over time damages the small blood vessels in the retina, the light-sensing layer at the back of the eye. Vessels can leak, close off, or be replaced by fragile new vessels that bleed. Fluid can also collect in the macula (*diabetic macular edema*), blurring central vision. Diabetic retinopathy is a leading cause of vision loss in people with diabetes.

## Symptoms

Early stages usually have **no symptoms**. Later you may notice:

- Blurry or fluctuating vision
- Floaters or dark spots
- Dark or empty areas in your vision
- Trouble seeing colors

## Who is at risk

Anyone with type 1, type 2 or gestational diabetes. The risk rises the longer you have diabetes and with high blood sugar, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, smoking and pregnancy.

## How it is diagnosed

A comprehensive **dilated eye exam** lets the doctor see the retina. Retinal photographs, optical coherence tomography (OCT) and fluorescein angiography show leaks and swelling in detail.

## Treatment

- **Managing diabetes:** steady blood sugar, blood pressure and cholesterol slow damage
- **Anti-VEGF injections:** reduce macular swelling and abnormal vessels
- **Laser treatment:** seals leaks or shrinks abnormal vessels
- **Vitrectomy surgery:** removes blood or scar tissue from inside the eye

## Prevention

A yearly dilated eye exam is the single most effective step, because treatment works best before vision is affected. Use our [exam schedule tool](tools/exam-schedule.html) to set a reminder.
