## What floaters are

Floaters are small dark specks, threads or cobwebs that drift across your vision, most noticeable against a bright sky or a white wall. They are shadows cast on the retina by clumps in the **vitreous**, the clear gel that fills the eye. As we age, the vitreous becomes more liquid and clumps form.

## Flashes and posterior vitreous detachment

With age, the vitreous gel often shrinks and pulls away from the retina. This is a **posterior vitreous detachment (PVD)** and is very common after 50. It can cause a sudden new floater and brief flashes of light, especially in dim light or with eye movement.

PVD itself is usually harmless. But in some people, the gel pulls hard enough to **tear the retina**. Fluid can then pass through the tear and lift the retina off the back of the eye, a **retinal detachment**, which can cause permanent vision loss if not repaired quickly.

## Who is at higher risk of retinal detachment

- People who are very nearsighted
- People who have had cataract or other eye surgery
- People with a past eye injury
- People who have had a retinal detachment in the other eye or have a family history of it

## How it is diagnosed

An eye doctor dilates your pupils and examines the whole retina. Anyone with new floaters or flashes should be examined, ideally the same day, even if the symptoms seem mild.

## Treatment

- **Harmless floaters:** no treatment needed
- **Retinal tears:** sealed in the office with laser or freezing treatment to prevent detachment
- **Retinal detachment:** surgery, often within a day or two, to reattach the retina
