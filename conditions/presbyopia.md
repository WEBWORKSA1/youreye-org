## What it is

To focus on something close, a small muscle in the eye changes the shape of the flexible lens behind the pupil. With age, the lens becomes stiffer and can no longer change shape enough. Close objects then stay blurry. This is presbyopia, and it happens to everyone.

## Symptoms

- Holding books, menus and phones farther away to read them
- Needing brighter light to read
- Eye strain or headaches after close work
- Blur when switching focus from far to near

## Who is affected

Everyone, usually from their early to mid 40s. People who are farsighted may notice it earlier. Nearsighted people may find they can read by taking their glasses off.

## How it is diagnosed

A routine eye exam tests your near vision and works out an **ADD** power, the extra reading strength added to your prescription. Try our [near vision card](tools/near-vision.html) to see where you stand.

## Treatment options

- **Reading glasses**, ready-made if your distance vision is good and both eyes are similar
- **Bifocals**, with a visible line between distance and near zones
- **Progressive lenses**, which change power gradually from distance to near with no line
- **Office or computer lenses** designed for desk and screen distances
- **Multifocal or monovision contact lenses**
- **Prescription eye drops** that temporarily shrink the pupil to improve near focus for some hours
- **Surgery**, such as monovision laser correction or refractive lens exchange, for some people

## Living with presbyopia

Good lighting, larger text on devices and keeping a pair of readers where you need them make daily life easier. Your near prescription will likely change every couple of years until about 60. The years after 40 are also when early signs of glaucoma and other diseases begin to appear, so keep up regular eye exams.
