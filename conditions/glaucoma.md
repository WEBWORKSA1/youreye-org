## What it is

Glaucoma is a group of eye diseases that damage the **optic nerve**, the cable that carries images from the eye to the brain. Damage is often linked to raised pressure inside the eye, but not always. It usually affects side (peripheral) vision first and spreads slowly toward the center.

## Symptoms

**Open-angle glaucoma**, the most common type, has no symptoms early on. There is no pain, and vision seems normal until a lot of nerve damage has happened. Many people do not know they have it.

**Angle-closure glaucoma** can come on suddenly when fluid suddenly cannot drain from the eye. It causes severe eye pain, redness, blurred vision, halos, headache and nausea. This is an emergency.

## Who is at risk

- Everyone over 60
- People with a parent or sibling with glaucoma
- Black and African American people over 40, and Hispanic and Latino people over 60, according to the National Eye Institute
- People with diabetes, high nearsightedness, a past eye injury or long-term steroid use

## How it is diagnosed

A comprehensive dilated eye exam looks at the optic nerve. Tests can include eye pressure (tonometry), a visual field test, corneal thickness and an OCT scan of the nerve fibres. Screening for eye pressure alone is not enough.

## Treatment

Treatment lowers eye pressure to protect the optic nerve:

- **Eye drops**, used every day
- **Laser treatment**, such as selective laser trabeculoplasty (SLT), which helps fluid drain
- **Surgery**, including minimally invasive glaucoma surgery, trabeculectomy and drainage implants

Taking drops exactly as prescribed is the most important part. Our [eye drops tips in the videos library](videos.html) can help.

## Prevention

You cannot prevent glaucoma, but you can prevent much of the vision loss it causes. If you are at higher risk, have a dilated eye exam every one to two years, or as your eye doctor recommends.
